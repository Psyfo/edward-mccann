# Photography & Art Direction Analysis

## The collection (observed)

~470 images across 27 projects, four eras/qualities visibly mixed:

1. **Recent professional architectural photography** (the asset): Lyndon Douglas (Firs Avenue, Oval Road, Latimer Road, Victualler, Highpoint), Emma Lewis (Ibberton), Travis Levius (Lonsdale Square, Pearson St, Sourced), Sebastian Tiew (Union Wharf). Character: overcast-soft London light, patient frontal or one-point compositions, honest colour (no HDR theatrics), inhabited-but-unpeopled rooms, garden foliage as framing device, genuine material detail work (charred timber grain, ply edges, marble counters).
2. **Older project photography**: flatter, small-camera work (e.g. the Victualler street elevation with parked cars, visible HDR sky); competent record shots rather than art direction.
3. **CAD/visualisation output**: line-work axonometrics with white grounds (Goat Hill, Social House, Hackney Road, Culford Mews), plus rendered views of varying vintage (Pennard's render era vs its brief). Some visualisations credit-worthy (the practice collaborates with Squint Opera and Atelier For Images per About).
4. **Physical model and process photography**: Campden House's silvered card model, Willow Tree's parabolic model: charismatic objects, photographed adequately.

## Current treatment (observed)

- No captions, no media labels (photo vs render vs model), no sequencing logic; galleries open with whatever was uploaded first.
- Thumbnails: hand-made square PNGs, some letterboxed with transparency, several with award badges or broadcaster logos composited in.
- Sizing: capped at a 945/975px column; no full-bleed usage anywhere; portraits shrink to ~55% width mid-gallery.
- Colour: unmanaged: warm interiors beside blue-cast exteriors beside paper-white CAD sheets.
- People: essentially absent across the entire collection (one or two incidental figures); no portrait of Edward McCann, no studio, no hands/making imagery.

## Interpretation

- The recent photography is **brand-grade**: Lyndon Douglas and Emma Lewis shoot for exactly the calibre of practice McCann's evidence suggests it is; the imagery would hold its own on any comparator site in this research. The site's presentation (small, uncaptioned, badge-stamped, column-bound) systematically spends this asset at a fraction of its value.
- The mixed media (drawings, models, renders) is not a weakness to hide but an unexploited narrative device: the practice's own copy walks from concept to construction ("nose to tail"); the image collection can literally show that arc, but only if media types are labelled and sequenced.
- The absence of human presence and studio imagery keeps the brand impersonal in exactly the dimension (an eponymous practice) where person is the product.

## What an ideal future direction should amplify (recommendation)

1. **Sequence as narrative**: per project: establishing context → the architectural move → inhabited spaces → material details → drawing/model evidence. The Tuckey comparator proves captioned mixed sequences read as editorial rigour.
2. **Scale contrast**: earn one full-bleed moment per project (the collection already contains images that sustain it) against contained supporting images; kill the uniform column.
3. **Declared media**: caption every image; distinguish photograph / visualisation / model / drawing explicitly: this converts the built-vs-unbuilt ambiguity from a trust risk into a transparency signal.
4. **Colour management**: grade toward a consistent temperature per project set; keep CAD sheets on the paper tone of the site so drawings feel native, not pasted.
5. **Commission the gaps**: portrait(s) of Edward McCann, studio/working imagery, and re-shoots or edits for the 2-3 projects represented only by dated record shots. Photographer credits become structured data.
6. **Thumbnail art direction**: one cover image per project chosen for the archive grid, cropped to a single system ratio (or a deliberate mixed-ratio rhythm), always badge-free.

## Unknowns

- Whether original full-resolution files beyond the CMS uploads exist with the practice or photographers (archive holds the CMS-hosted maxima, largest ~3.6 MB PNGs and ~2500px JPEGs).
