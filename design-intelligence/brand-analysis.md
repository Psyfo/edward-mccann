# Brand Analysis

The most consequential file in this package alongside `design-handoff.md`. Every descriptor below is tied to observed evidence; interpretation and recommendation are separated.

## What the brand consists of today (observed)

1. A typeset all-caps wordmark: EDWARD McCANN ARCHITECTURE in futura-pt regular, with "Mc" set naturalistically (lowercase c). No mark, no symbol, no lockups, no logo file. The favicon/touch icon contains a cropped stencil "EM" monogram fragment: someone once gestured at a mark and abandoned it.
2. A serif voice: adobe-garamond-pro everywhere else, italic carrying navigation state and titles.
3. A near-white ground (#F7F7F7), black ink, photography as the only colour.
4. A body of work whose photography (recent projects) is genuinely strong, and whose writing is literate, specific and unpretentious.
5. Institutional credentials (RIBA, ARB), an elite education/employment lineage (Cambridge, AA, Adjaye Associates, Edward Cullinan), award recognition (Don't Move Improve 2017/2019/2022, Grand Designs TV + Magazine, a public-art competition win), and a London + Cape Town footprint. All present, all buried.

## Brand personality (evidence-tested descriptors)

| Descriptor | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Restrained | **Yes, genuinely** | No hero slogans, no sales adjectives anywhere in 27 project texts; monochrome chrome; the practice never says "luxury", "bespoke" or "award-winning" in running copy |
| Intellectual / referential | **Yes** | Copy cites Sverre Fehn's Nordic Pavilion, Josef Albers, Lubetkin/Tecton, the Eames Eiffel base; describes medieval garden geometry for a pergola |
| Human / client-first | **Yes** | "It is the job of your architect to reveal these possibilities to you"; process described as conversation; colleagues and collaborators named |
| Tactile / material | **Yes in copy and photography** | Charred timber, rammed earth, limed birch ply, precast coloured concrete recur; detail photography exists |
| Editorial | **No (aspiration visible, execution absent)** | Serif + italic gestures at editorial; justified 120-char lines, uncaptioned image dumps and badge clutter destroy it |
| Institutional | **Partially** | RIBA/ARB logos and process language, but no governance/people layer |
| Contemporary | **No** | Bootstrap-era layout, 2018 CMS, Font Awesome icons, baked-in badges read as a decade old |
| Luxurious / premium | **No** | Nothing signals fee level; presentation undersells work that is objectively high-end (Grade II* listed interventions, Somerset estates, Grand Designs coverage) |
| Experimental | **Trace** | Folly, Chamber, Willow Tree and the Kew entry show a conceptual side the architecture of the site hides |
| Commercial/residential balance | **Hidden** | Hospitality and workplace projects exist but are undiscoverable as a category |

**Net observed personality**: a thoughtful, materially serious, quietly confident author whose website communicates "small practice that had a website made in 2018 and stopped tending it".

## Brand positioning

- **Who does this practice appear to be?** (Observed) An East London RIBA practice doing residential remodels and extensions, with unexplained excursions into wine bars, sculpture and furniture. (Reality per evidence) A pedigreed studio operating across London and the English countryside, plus Cape Town, with an unusually broad sensibility spanning buildings, interiors, objects and public art.
- **Implied level**: the presentation implies modest domestic work; the content (Grade I and II* listed contexts, estate commissions, TV coverage) implies a significantly higher tier. This gap IS the redesign brief.
- **Client it appears designed to attract**: a Hackney homeowner researching extensions. **Clients the evidence could support attracting**: country-house commissioners, hospitality operators, developers, cultural collaborators.
- **Differentiators available (all currently under-leveraged)**:
  1. "Nose to tail design": an ownable, personality-rich philosophy phrase already in the copy.
  2. The mixed portfolio (houses + hospitality + objects + art) as one sensibility, not noise.
  3. Adjaye/Cullinan/SCABAL lineage + Cambridge/AA education.
  4. London + Cape Town dual geography.
  5. Writing quality: the practice can publish like an author, not a vendor.
  6. Recognition record incl. Grand Designs television.

## Emotional impression

- **Observed** (what a visitor actually meets): a wall of small mixed images; serif text walls; silence about who, where, how much, what kind. Award stickers as the loudest visual element on the page.
- **Interpretation** (what that likely makes them feel): mild interest, then effort, then uncertainty ("are they still active?", "is this their level?", "do they do my kind of project?"). The dead analytics, 2019-frozen press page and lorem ipsum reinforce dormancy for anyone who looks closely. Trust is carried entirely by the photographs and, for the persistent reader, the prose.
- **The feeling the evidence could support**: quiet authority; the sense of entering a working studio's well-kept archive; warmth without informality.

## Identity inconsistencies to resolve (observed)

- Five practice-name variants (Architecture / Architects / Architectural Practice / Ed McCann / Mccann).
- "studio" in the domain but nowhere in the brand language.
- Italic = active nav state, but also = titles, also = card labels: italics mean three things.
- Awards live as raster badges in thumbnails, not as brand-controlled information.

## Recommendations (for the design model)

1. Treat the redesign as **repositioning through presentation**, not reinvention: the personality (restrained, literate, material, human) is real and evidenced; the site must stop contradicting it.
2. Resolve the name. Evidence base: domain is `edwardmccann.studio`; wordmark says ARCHITECTURE; body says Architects. Any choice works; one choice must win everywhere (a decision to put to the client, flagged in open questions).
3. Preserve as brand DNA: the caps-geometric wordmark instinct, the serif literary voice, the near-white ground, "nose to tail design", the naming of collaborators and photographers.
4. Elevate to first-class brand elements: project facts, recognition record, the dual-city footprint, the conceptual side (Folly/Chamber/Willow Tree).
5. The latent "EM" monogram is a legitimate thread to explore properly (see logo implications in `design-handoff.md`), not an obligation.
