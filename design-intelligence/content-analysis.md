# Content Analysis

Companion to `copy-inventory.md` (which holds the verbatim record). This file evaluates the content as strategy.

## Voice (observed → interpretation)

- Long, controlled sentences; concrete nouns; almost no adjectives of praise. Materials are always named precisely (charred timber, limed birch ply, rammed earth, precast coloured concrete, sheet-steel stair). Interpretation: the writing performs competence instead of claiming it: rare and valuable.
- Second-person warmth in About ("you the client are invited into the process to comment, steer and ultimately sign off"). Interpretation: a practice comfortable demystifying itself; strong fit for private clients undertaking "a once in a lifetime project" (their phrase).
- Cultural references (Fehn, Albers, Lubetkin, Eames, medieval garden geometry) appear casually, without name-dropping tone. Interpretation: the intellectual register is native, not performed.
- UK English throughout; no marketing vocabulary anywhere ("luxury", "bespoke", "stunning" never appear). This restraint is a brand asset in a category drowning in "considered" and "crafted" (see `competitive-positioning.md`).

## Content strengths worth preserving nearly verbatim

1. "We believe in nose to tail design..." (positioning statement, ownable).
2. The About PROJECTS and PROCESS paragraphs (fix formatting, keep language).
3. 19 substantive project descriptions; the best (Firs Avenue, Pennard House, Lonsdale Square, Union Wharf, Victualler, Willow Tree, Kew) need no rewriting at all.
4. Photographer credits culture (extend it to all projects).
5. Named colleagues and collaborators (convert to a real people/collaborators section).

## Where copy feels generic (rare)

- "Ultimately we have an interest in developing robust, well considered, environmentally conscious and beautiful buildings which leave our clients both satisfied and happy" is the one sentence that drifts toward category boilerplate ("well considered"). Everything else is specific.

## Missing content the rebuild will need (gap list for the client)

- Descriptions for the 8 bare projects; year/status/location/team facts for all 27.
- A short authored practice statement for the homepage (the About intro can seed it).
- Portrait + studio photography; a sentence on the Cape Town presence and how the two cities work.
- Recognition list with dates and outlets (DMI 2017, DMI Featured 2019, DMI Longlisted 2022 for Hassett Road, Grand Designs TV 2019 for West Suffolk, Grand Designs Magazine feature for Latimer Road, Willow Tree competition win, Kew entry).
- Image captions, at minimum distinguishing photo / render / model / drawing and naming rooms or moves; the existing prose proves the author can write them well.
- Alt text per image (can be derived from captions).

## SEO / discoverability content reality (observed)

- Title patterns inconsistent; no OG tags at all (shares render bare); no structured data; no sitemap; meta descriptions good-to-missing; one h1 missing on home/contact, three h1s on Press.
- The site currently ranks on brand name only. The rebuild inherits near-zero technical SEO to preserve, which is freeing: only the 31 public URLs need redirect mapping.

## Recommendation

Adopt an "edit, don't rewrite" content strategy: fix defects, add structure (facts blocks, captions), commission the missing 8 descriptions in the existing voice, and lift the About credentials into designed components. The voice itself is the single strongest brand asset after the photography and must survive the redesign intact.
