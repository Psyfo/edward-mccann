# Recommended Creative Territories

Three genuinely different foundations, each grounded in evidence from this audit and the comparator research. These are territories for the design model to develop, not designs. The final section recommends one.

---

## Territory A: The Working Catalogue

**Concept**: the practice as a rigorous, numbered archive; 27 works as an index that grows for decades. Lineage: Al-Jawad Pike's coded index, 31/44's Selected/Catalogue split, reframed for McCann.

- **Emotional qualities**: rigour, permanence, quiet authority, collection-worthiness.
- **Visual characteristics**: systematised caps wordmark; project codes and metadata as a visible typographic layer; curated Selected grid (mixed, art-directed ratios) above a complete sortable text index (name / place / year / type / status); monochrome chrome.
- **Typography**: one rigorously used family (a characterful neutral grotesque OR a sharp contemporary serif) with real weights; tabular numerals doing identity work.
- **Colour**: paper white + ink black, tuned; at most one material-derived accent.
- **Imagery**: one disciplined cover per project; sequences tightly edited; drawings as plates.
- **Layout**: strict columnar grid, index tables as a design feature.
- **Motion**: minimal; index sorting/filtering as the signature interaction.
- **Brand personality**: precise, institutional-in-the-good-sense, collectible.
- **Advantages**: converts small-practice scale into editorial discipline; extremely durable; flatters the mixed portfolio by classifying it.
- **Risks**: coldness (fights the warm client-first voice); the numbered-archive move is already AJP's signature in this exact scene; needs impeccable execution to avoid pastiche.
- **Suitability**: high for peers/press; medium for private clients choosing a companion for a once-in-a-lifetime project.

## Territory B: Nose to Tail (The Studio Notebook)

**Concept**: concept-to-construction storytelling as the identity: every project told the way the practice already writes: idea, references, material decisions, junctions, credits. The site behaves like a working notebook made public. Lineage: Tuckey's captioned editorial depth + Studio McW's named-principles brand argument, fused with McCann's own phrase.

- **Emotional qualities**: warmth, intelligence, craft, trust, appetite (the phrase is culinary; East London St. John-culture resonance).
- **Visual characteristics**: editorial page architecture: full-bleed openings earned per project, captioned figures, drawings and models beside photographs as equals, marginalia-like metadata; the old-new dialogue of the buildings mirrored in typographic contrast.
- **Typography**: serif-led reading voice (recommitted properly: real cuts, weights, small caps, ragged right) with a geometric caps counter-voice for structure/wayfinding: evolving the existing Garamond/Futura DNA rather than replacing it.
- **Colour**: warm paper + near-black ink; material accents drawn from the work (charred timber, rammed earth, oxide) used sparingly in rules/marks.
- **Imagery**: sequenced narratives (context → move → inhabitation → detail → drawing); declared media labels; construction/process imagery promoted, not hidden.
- **Layout**: a single reading-grid system spanning essay and archive; generous but rhythmic whitespace.
- **Motion**: editorial pacing only: restrained reveals, a considered end-of-story handoff to the next project.
- **Brand personality**: the literate maker; confident without volume.
- **Advantages**: builds directly on the practice's two proven assets (writing, photography) and its actual philosophy phrase; unifies the mixed portfolio as one sensibility; almost nobody in the category captions or shows process, so depth = differentiation; survives content phases (works at 8 projects deep or 27).
- **Risks**: requires the content sprint (captions, facts, 8 missing texts); serif-led warmth can drift twee if the grid isn't strict; "notebook" must not become scrapbook.
- **Suitability**: high for private clients and hospitality commissioners; strong for press; authentic to the person.

## Territory C: The Quiet Monograph

**Concept**: the practice as author; the site as a slowly accreting book about one architect's way of seeing. Lineage: William Smalley's model, McCann-flavoured (his voice is warmer and more practical than Smalley's rarefied register).

- **Emotional qualities**: stillness, permanence, connoisseurship.
- **Visual characteristics**: one image at a time; long uninterrupted sequences; text as considered essays; a Record-like diary of press, notes, collaborations.
- **Typography**: a single beautiful serif doing everything, large sizes, book conventions.
- **Colour**: strict paper/ink; photography as the only event.
- **Imagery**: fewer, larger, sequenced; film-photography sensibility.
- **Motion**: near none; page-turn pacing.
- **Brand personality**: the architect as essayist.
- **Advantages**: maximal differentiation from portfolio-grid convention; matches an eponymous practice; the writing could sustain it.
- **Risks**: demands sustained authorship (the practice's track record of site upkeep is the counter-evidence: press frozen since 2019); hides the breadth (hospitality/commercial) that wins other work; reputation-led model suits a practice further into its public arc.
- **Suitability**: high-character, high-risk; strongest once the practice commits to publishing regularly.

---

## Recommendation: Territory B, "Nose to Tail", with A's index as its archive layer

**Why B**: it is the only territory whose core materials already exist and are verified (the philosophy phrase, the literate texts, the sequencable image collections, the mixed typologies needing a unifying frame). It amplifies rather than replaces the observed brand DNA (serif voice, caps counterpoint, warm monochrome), directly answers the audit's biggest failures (undifferentiated dumping of good material), and occupies the least-crowded credible position in the comparator landscape (editorial depth with captions and process). It also de-risks content phasing: a notebook admits unfinished entries; a catalogue demands completeness and a monograph demands literature.

**Why keep A's index inside it**: the two-tier archive (curated Selected + complete Index with real metadata) is the proven mechanism that makes 27 heterogeneous projects read as discipline; it slots into B as the "contents page" of the notebook without importing A's coldness.

**Why not C now**: publishing-cadence risk contradicted by the site's own history; revisit as an evolution once the practice demonstrates appetite for the journal layer.

**Signature moves available to B** (for the design model to test, not mandates): the ">>" conversation mark from the About copy as a drawn brand device; the resolved "EM" monogram thread from the abandoned favicon; sector labels in the practice's own plain vocabulary (Houses / Places to eat and drink / Objects / Public work); recognition set as text, never badges; the Cape Town axis expressed as two place-datelines in the footer.
